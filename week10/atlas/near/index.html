Data scope and reuse

Of the 110 labelled activations, 105 belong to the eight states; the other 5 are unknown, kept on the video timeline only, with no ninth state. Included: 31 Stationary, 14 Left, 18 Right, 1 Forward, 5 Back, 13 Forehand, 17 Backhand, 6 Serve. Every sequence is kept; no single representative is picked per action.

Reuses the original identity, timing, speed attributes, skeletons and existing pixels, and exports the clips not exported before. The 420 original Left → Forehand / Backhand judgements are kept with unchanged pair values; the new entry / settle transitions use separately marked phase criteria. Labels are accurate to about ±0.15 s; 28 forehands / backhands have no exact internal phases and keep their full start.

The near player in white (02/03/04/06) has no matching action labels and is not mixed in. Slow back-steps take part as variants; clips with severe pixel failures or missing frames are shown but not used for control.Download full data ↗

Method and conventions

Movement: start → running core repeats → exit. Stationary: enter → small-motion core repeats (short clips hold a stable pose) → leave. A held input within one state repeats only the current clip’s core and never switches clips. Across states, an existing legal exit in the current core or tail is used; strokes play in full.

Natural source evidence and runtime transitions are kept apart: being adjacent in the source does not make an aligned pixel seam acceptable. All composite atlases are made offline; the browser only looks up and plays them. This is a 2D research demo: no guarantee of real foot contact, ball contact or physical speed.

Control coverage and current limits

An input never creates a missing transition; without a direct edge it may pass through a clearly shown non-stroke action. Actions, seams and exits all come from stored assets. Strokes cannot be interrupted or chained directly into another stroke; Serve starts only from Stationary with no movement request; losing focus clears input and pauses.

The player is a 2D motion demo at a fixed root position, not a full-court rally. Variety is limited by the compatibility graph; Forward has only one source clip and no more are invented. Not yet visually accepted frame by frame: masks may have gaps, edges and seams may ghost; known severe failures are excluded from control.
